新发呼吸道传染病：快速识别和管理的临床方面。

Emerging respiratory infectious diseases: Clinical aspects for rapid identification and management.

Emerging respiratory infectious diseases increasingly arise at the human-animal-environment interface and challenge clinicians with rapidly evolving diagnostic, therapeutic, and infection-control decisions. This narrative review summarizes clinically relevant viral and bacterial pathogens that may present as severe respiratory disease, but are easily mistaken for common community-acquired infections during early illness. We focus on zoonotic influenza A viruses, including contemporary H5N1, Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV), hantaviruses, Nipah virus, and inhalational bacterial threats such as anthrax, pneumonic plague, tularemia, and melioidosis. These infections may progress from non-specific symptoms to severe pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, shock, neurological complications, disseminated infection, or death. Timely recognition depends on careful assessment of travel, occupational and recreational exposures, animal contact, outbreak signals, and pathogen-specific syndromic clues. Multiplex molecular diagnostics can support early triage and exclusion of common pathogens, but targeted PCR, culture, serology, sequencing, and reference laboratory confirmation remain essential. Management requires prompt supportive care, appropriate isolation, public health notification, and pathogen-directed antimicrobial, antiviral, antitoxin, or prophylactic strategies where available. By integrating clinical presentation, diagnostics, treatment, vaccines, and One Health considerations, this review aims to translate knowledge on emerging infections into practical bedside preparedness.

Hantavirus in Dentistry: Occupational Exposure Considerations and a Proposed Tiered Infection Prevention and Control Framework-A Narrative Review.

The aim of this study is to synthesize evidence on hantavirus transmission and occupational exposure pathways relevant to dentistry, and to propose an evidence-informed tiered infection prevention and control (IPC) framework that distinguishes established from theoretical risk. The initial narrative search covered database inception through May 15, 2026 in MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar, and WHO/CDC/ECDC/OSHA sources, followed by a targeted PubMed/MEDLINE and public-health guidance update through August 18, 2026. Approximately 1450 records were identified. After duplicate removal, 1070 records were subjected to title/abstract screening, 215 full-text records were assessed for eligibility, and 45 sources were included in the final synthesis, comprising 34 peer-reviewed publications and 11 gray-literature or public-health guidance sources. Environmental exposure to infected-rodent contamination is the established occupational pathway. Limited person-to-person transmission is documented for the Andes virus (ANDV), but no dental transmission has been reported. ANDV RNA and culture-based infectivity have been demonstrated in some oral/respiratory fluids; however, dental-aerosol transmission has not been shown. Dental-specific occupational risk therefore remains unquantified. No dental transmission has been documented, and patient-related risk remains uncertain. Standard precautions and rodent control are foundational; suspected or confirmed ANDV warrants transmission-based precautions and airborne precautions for aerosol-generating procedures according to current guidance. The proposed dental tiers are evidence-informed, author-derived, and unvalidated.

汉坦病毒心肺综合征：一种新出现的致命人畜共患感染。

汉坦病毒心肺综合征是由新大陆汉坦病毒引起的一种潜在致命的呼吸道人畜共患感染，主要通过吸入受感染啮齿动物的雾化排泄物而获得。在过去的 30 年里，HCPS 已经超出了最初的焦点，最近的事件表明有可能蔓延到传统流行地区之外。尽管采取了最佳的支持治疗，该疾病仍会从模糊的前驱症状迅速发展为急性心肺衰竭，死亡率很高。病理生理学涉及内皮衰竭、血管通透性增加、急性血小板减少症和导致毛细血管渗漏综合征和心源性休克的过度免疫反应。由于前驱症状模糊且反映了常见的病毒感染和呼吸系统疾病，因此临床上仍然很难及早怀疑。诊断确认需要血清学检测或遗传学方法，这在资源有限的流行情况下通常无法实现，从而导致相当大的诊断延误。管理通常是支持性的，包括早期入住 ICU、明智的水化管理、机械呼吸和心肺支持，包括难治性患者的体外膜肺氧合。最近 2026 年 5 月在一艘游轮上发生的多国安第斯山脉病毒疫情凸显了 HCPS 在通常的流行环境之外在全球范围内快速传播的可能性。本综述总结了有关 HCPS 流行病学、病理生理学、临床表现、诊断技术和治疗方案的最新信息。我们还强调，迫切需要改善全球监测、快速即时诊断、提高医护人员的全球意识，以及不断开发预防性疫苗和有效的抗病毒疗法，以应对这一重大的公共卫生威胁。

Hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome: an emerging fatal zoonotic infection.

Hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome is a potentially fatal respiratory zoonotic infection caused by New World hantaviruses, and it is acquired mostly via inhaling aerosolized excreta from infected rodents. Over the past 30 years, HCPS has expanded beyond its initial foci, and recent events suggest a potential for spread beyond traditionally endemic regions. The disease rapidly progresses from a vague prodrome to acute cardiopulmonary failure with a high fatality rate despite optimum supportive treatment. Pathophysiology involves endothelial failure, increased vascular permeability, acute thrombocytopenia and excessive immunological responses resulting in capillary leak syndrome and cardiogenic shock. It is still hard to clinically suspect early since the prodromal symptoms are vague and mirror common viral infections and respiratory disorders. Diagnostic confirmation needs serological testing or genetic approaches, which are typically unavailable in resource-limited endemic situations, leading to considerable diagnostic delays. Management is generally supportive, with early ICU admission, judicious hydration management, mechanical breathing and cardiopulmonary support including extracorporeal membrane oxygenation in refractory patients. The recent multi-country Andes virus epidemic on a cruise ship in May 2026 highlights the potential for fast worldwide transmission of HCPS outside of usual endemic settings. This review summarizes the current information on the epidemiology, pathophysiology, clinical presentation, diagnostic techniques, and treatment options for HCPS. We also highlight the pressing need for improved global surveillance, rapid point-of-care diagnostics, global awareness among health care workers, and ongoing development of preventive vaccines and effective antiviral therapies to address this significant public health threat.

Multi-Country Human-to-Human Andes Virus Outbreak on a Cruise Ship: A Disaster Report on Prehospital, Health Care Facility, and Public Health System Challenges - Essential Knowledge for Frontline Clinicians.

The Andes Virus (ANDV) outbreak reported on May 2, 2026, aboard a luxury vessel departing from Ushuaia, Patagonia, Argentina, was caused by an Orthohantavirus unique in its person-to-person transmissibility in contrast to zoonotic transmission characteristic of better-known hantaviruses. Containment required coordinated international management because passengers and crew embarked and disembarked at several international ports-of-call prior to outbreak recognition, resulting in cross-border transmission potential. Among those who disembarked, several traveled on flights to various destinations. Cases subsequently manifested in multiple countries, including Tristan da Cunha, Spain, France, Switzerland, South Africa, Canada, and The Netherlands. Upon notification, international health authorities implemented containment measures, such as contact tracing, quarantine, and isolation. Uniquely, ANDV poses distinctive risks due to its immediately prodromal presymptomatic transmission potential, initial non-specific flu-like symptoms, prolonged incubation period, conceivable long-term neurologic and endocrinologic sequalae, high case fatality rates (CFRs), and possibility of international dissemination. This report describes the successful management of a multi-national outbreak associated with cruise-ship travel, a setting with unique challenges. It also examines implications and challenges for prehospital, health care facility, and public health systems for future ANDV outbreaks. The event was characterized by delayed recognition, international spread, and complex coordination across multiple jurisdictions and sectors, including medical and public health authorities, policy- and decision-makers, crisis and emergency risk-communication experts, media, logistics, transportation, and security. Rapid implementation of public health measures coupled with clinician implementation of the Identify-Isolate-Inform (3I) model, a clinical framework to detect and prevent the spread of infectious disease, at the prehospital and health care facility levels are critical actions that can contain future ANDV outbreaks.

汉坦病毒肺综合征的血浆交换：未经测试的干预措施的机制案例。

汉坦病毒肺综合征 (HPS) 的病死率为 35%-40%，且尚无批准的药物治疗方法。其病理生理学根源于血浆室：细胞因子、病毒抗原、免疫复合物和血小板结合糖蛋白作为免疫病理性内皮损伤、非心源性肺水肿和心源性抑郁的驱动因素而循环。治疗性血浆置换 (TPE) 可实现血浆介质介质的大量清除，由美国血浆置换协会 (ASFA) 建立，用于具有类似机制原理的众多适应症。该评论认为，HPS 的免疫病理学映射到 TPE 的治疗靶点，任何干预都需要在早期心肺期进行，并且该疾病的凝血病维度为将血浆纳入替代策略提供了机制论据。我们特意将这一基本原理与类似病理生理学疾病中 TPE 的不均匀记录进行对比，在这些疾病中，干预措施在某些情况下有效，但在其他情况下没有明显的益处。最近，MV Hondius 号游轮上爆发了安第斯山脉病毒，造成了多国严重心肺病例，促使我们作为输血医学和单采医生阐明了这一假设。我们强调，这些论点是机械论和假设生成的，而不是基于证据的：尚无临床数据支持 HPS 中的 TPE，并且在得出任何关于疗效的结论之前，需要系统的结果报告（最好是通过多中心病例系列）。

Plasma Exchange for Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome: A Mechanistic Case for an Untested Intervention.

Hantavirus pulmonary syndrome (HPS) carries a case fatality rate of 35%-40% and has no approved pharmacologic treatment. Its pathophysiology is rooted in the plasma compartment: cytokines, viral antigens, immune complexes, and platelet-binding glycoproteins circulate as drivers of immunopathologic endothelial injury, noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, and cardiogenic depression. Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) achieves bulk clearance of plasma-borne mediators and is established by the American Society for Apheresis (ASFA) for numerous indications that share an analogous mechanistic rationale. This commentary argues that the immunopathology of HPS maps onto the therapeutic targets of TPE, that any intervention would need to be timed to the early cardiopulmonary phase, and that the coagulopathic dimension of the disease provides a mechanistic argument for incorporating plasma into the replacement strategy. We deliberately situate this rationale against the uneven record of TPE in diseases of comparable pathophysiology, in which the intervention has been effective in some settings and without demonstrable benefit in others. The recent Andes virus outbreak aboard the cruise ship MV Hondius, which produced a multinational cluster of severe cardiopulmonary cases, prompted us, as transfusion medicine and apheresis physicians, to articulate this hypothesis. We emphasize that these arguments are mechanistic and hypothesis-generating rather than evidence-based: no clinical data yet support TPE in HPS, and systematic outcome reporting, ideally through a multicenter case series, would be required before any conclusion about efficacy could be drawn.
