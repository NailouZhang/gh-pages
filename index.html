学术文献 · headline

Design and in silico evaluation of PV-GNProtecta: an mRNA multi-epitope vaccine candidate against Puumala virus using immunoinformatics and structural analysis.

Puumala virus (PUUV), the principal causative agent of nephropathia epidemica (NE), poses a significant public health threat due to its zoonotic transmission, renal complications, and the lack of an approved vaccine. In this study, an immunoinformatics-driven mRNA multi-epitope vaccine, PV-GNProtecta, was designed using conserved regions of the viral glycoprotein (G) and nucleoprotein (N). Highly antigenic, non-allergenic, and non-toxic T-cell and B-cell epitopes were identified and assembled with appropriate adjuvants to enhance immunogenicity. Population coverage analysis suggested broad HLA responsiveness across diverse populations. The vaccine construct exhibited favorable physicochemical properties, including stability, solubility, and antigenicity. Structural validation predicted high model quality, with 95.5% of residues located in favored Ramachandran regions and an ERRAT score of 91.4. Molecular docking and MM-GBSA analyses predicted favorable computational interactions between PV-GNProtecta and TLR4, while a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation provided computational insights into the dynamic behavior of the vaccine-TLR4 complex. Codon optimization suggested compatibility of the designed sequence with an Escherichia coli-based expression framework. Immune simulation predicted potential humoral and cellular immune response patterns, including changes in IgG, IFN-γ, and IL-2 levels. The designed mRNA sequence exhibited favorable predicted secondary structural characteristics; however, further experimental evaluation is required to assess translational efficiency and stability. Although this study is limited to computational analyses and requires experimental validation, the findings suggest that PV-GNProtecta may represent a potential candidate for further investigation against PUUV infection.

Hantavirus emergence through a One Health lens: ecological dynamics, human exposure and integrated surveillance

Abstract

Hantaviruses are rodent-borne zoonotic viruses that cause haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome in Eurasia and hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome in the Americas. Although human hantavirus infections are usually recognised clinically, infection risk is affected by upstream factors including reservoir-host ecology, environmental variability and human exposure. Evidence from China, Europe and the Americas shows that the incidence of hantavirus may depend on rodent population dynamics, climate conditions, land use, agricultural practices, housing environments and recreational or travel-related exposure. In China, the seasonal pattern of haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome, including a major autumn–winter peak and a smaller spring or spring–summer peak in many endemic areas, illustrates how virus–host ecology and agricultural labour jointly affect the human risk. In Europe, bank vole population cycles have been shown to influence Puumala virus epidemics, while studies in the Americas have linked rainfall, rodent abundance and human exposure in domestic, occupational and recreational settings. This mini review synthesises evidence on the ecological drivers, exposure pathways and surveillance challenges for hantavirus diseases through a One Health lens. We suggest that One Health approaches should move beyond general calls for multisector collaboration towards evidence-informed surveillance linking rodent, environmental and human data to setting-specific prevention, such as issuing seasonal risk warnings and reinforcing exposure-reduction measures before harvest periods in endemic agricultural regions.

Vaccines Against Hantavirus Infections: A Systematic Literature Review

Background/Objectives: Hantaviruses infections are responsible for a hemorrhagic fever with renal and cardiopulmonary syndrome with case-fatality rates up to 50%. While inactivated vaccines are already available in China and South Korea, several additional candidate vaccines are under development. This systematic review summarizes the current clinical evidence on immunogenicity and short-term safety of vaccines targeting different hantavirus strains. Methods: Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, a systematic literature search was carried out to identify randomized and nonrandomized trials of any phase. Data were synthesized narratively within strata defined by vaccine platform, target virus, assay, immunogenicity and safety. Results: Ten trials on 961 adults were included: four single-arm trials on the inactivated vaccine and six early-phase DNA-vaccine trials. One month after last dose, the inactivated vaccine achieved neutralizing seropositivity by PRNT50 in 45.1% of the tested subjects after two priming doses and 55.7% after three, while the corresponding binding-antibody values measured by IFA were 87.3% and 95.7%. Among DNA vaccines, homologous responses reached 100% against HTNV and 83.3% against PUUV with monovalent constructs, whereas dual responses to the bivalent construct were registered in 33.3% and 77.8% of the subjects, when measured through PRNT50 and PsVNA50, respectively. One serious adverse event occurred, most likely unrelated to vaccination. Conclusions: No serious safety signal was identified, although safety remains insufficiently characterized. Measured immunogenicity depends as much on the assay as on the vaccine, and, in the absence of a validated correlate of protection, it cannot be read as a clinical benefit. Future research should prioritize validated correlates of protection and innovative trial designs adapted to low-incidence diseases.
