[Preparedness for summer illnesses and emergencies in Chile, 2024-2025: lessons from an innovative policyPreparação para doenças e emergências no verão de 2024-2025 no Chile: lições de uma política inovadora].

Climate change and the increasing frequency of extreme heat events have heightened the importance of health issues during the summer season. This season is also associated with greater population mobility, outdoor recreational activities, and exposure to biological and environmental risks, all of which require coordinated responses from health systems. Within this context, the Ministry of Health of Chile implemented the summer seasonal illness and emergency preparedness plan 2024-2025, with the goal of strengthening surveillance, intersectoral coordination, and risk communication in response to priority threats to population health.This article describes the design, implementation, and key results of this first national initiative to prepare for diseases occurring during the summer season. The strategy was based on a multilevel governance model that liaised between the central, regional, and local levels of the health system through national coordination meetings, integrated surveillance, strengthening of technical capacities, and public communications. During the season covered by the plan, detection of Aedes aegypti remained confined to areas where it was previously known to occur; 395 outbreaks of foodborne illnesses affecting 2618 people were investigated; 8 cases of hantavirus were reported, with no deaths; and environmental risks and emergencies were monitored, including 4330 forest fires, none of which affected health facilities.This experience demonstrates the feasibility of implementing integrated disease preparedness strategies during the summer season and provides lessons for their consolidation as public policy, including the formalization of coordination mechanisms, the systematic assessment of implementation, and the incorporation of new hazards, such as drowning and injuries associated with recreational water activities. El cambio climático y la frecuencia creciente de eventos extremos asociados al calor han incrementado la relevancia de los temas de salud en la temporada estival. Esta, además, se asocia con una mayor movilidad poblacional, actividades recreativas al aire libre y exposición a riesgos biológicos y ambientales, lo que exige respuestas coordinadas por parte de los sistemas de salud. En este contexto, el Ministerio de Salud de Chile implementó el Plan de preparación para enfermedades y emergencias estacionales de verano 2024-2025, con el objetivo de fortalecer la vigilancia, la coordinación intersectorial y la comunicación de riesgo frente a amenazas prioritarias para la salud poblacional. Este artículo describe el diseño, la implementación y los principales resultados de esta primera experiencia nacional de preparación frente a las enfermedades durante la temporada estival. La estrategia se sustentó en un modelo de gobernanza multinivel que articuló los niveles central, regional y local del sistema de salud, mediante sesiones nacionales de coordinación, vigilancia integrada, fortalecimiento de capacidades técnicas y vocerías públicas. Durante su implementación, se mantuvo la detección de Aedes aegypti circunscrita a zonas previamente conocidas, se investigaron 395 brotes de enfermedades transmitidas por alimentos que afectaron a 2 618 personas, se notificaron 8 casos de hantavirus, sin fallecidos, y se realizó el seguimiento de riesgos ambientales y emergencias, incluidos 4 330 incendios forestales, sin afectación de establecimientos de salud. La experiencia demuestra la factibilidad de implementar estrategias integradas de preparación frente a las enfermedades durante la temporada estival y aporta lecciones para su consolidación como política pública, entre ellas la formalización de mecanismos de coordinación, la evaluación sistemática de la implementación y la incorporación de nuevos riesgos, como los ahogamientos y las lesiones recreacionales acuáticas. A mudança do clima e a crescente frequência de eventos extremos relacionados ao calor aumentaram a importância das questões de saúde durante o verão. Essa época também está associada a uma maior mobilidade populacional, atividades recreativas ao ar livre e exposição a riscos biológicos e ambientais, exigindo respostas coordenadas dos sistemas de saúde. Nesse contexto, o Ministério da Saúde do Chile implementou o Plano de preparação para doenças e emergências sazonais de verão 2024-2025, com o objetivo de fortalecer a vigilância, a coordenação intersetorial e a comunicação de riscos diante das principais ameaças à saúde pública.Este artigo descreve a concepção, a implementação e os principais resultados desta primeira experiência nacional de preparação para doenças durante o verão. A estratégia se baseou em um modelo de governança multinível que articulou os níveis central, regional e local do sistema de saúde, por meio de sessões de coordenação nacional, vigilância integrada, fortalecimento de capacidades técnicas e comunicações públicas. Durante sua implementação, a detecção do Aedes aegypti permaneceu restrita a áreas previamente conhecidas, foram investigados 395 surtos de doenças transmitidas por alimentos que afetaram 2618 pessoas, foram notificados 8 casos de hantavírus (sem óbitos) e foram monitorados os riscos ambientais e as emergências, incluindo 4330 incêndios florestais (nenhum dos quais afetou estabelecimentos de saúde).Essa experiência demonstra a viabilidade de implementar estratégias integradas de preparação para doenças durante o verão e fornece lições para sua consolidação como política pública – entre elas, a formalização de mecanismos de coordenação, a avaliação sistemática da implementação e a incorporação de novos riscos, como afogamentos e lesões relacionadas a atividades aquáticas recreativas.

Emerging respiratory infectious diseases: Clinical aspects for rapid identification and management.

Emerging respiratory infectious diseases increasingly arise at the human-animal-environment interface and challenge clinicians with rapidly evolving diagnostic, therapeutic, and infection-control decisions. This narrative review summarizes clinically relevant viral and bacterial pathogens that may present as severe respiratory disease, but are easily mistaken for common community-acquired infections during early illness. We focus on zoonotic influenza A viruses, including contemporary H5N1, Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV), hantaviruses, Nipah virus, and inhalational bacterial threats such as anthrax, pneumonic plague, tularemia, and melioidosis. These infections may progress from non-specific symptoms to severe pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, shock, neurological complications, disseminated infection, or death. Timely recognition depends on careful assessment of travel, occupational and recreational exposures, animal contact, outbreak signals, and pathogen-specific syndromic clues. Multiplex molecular diagnostics can support early triage and exclusion of common pathogens, but targeted PCR, culture, serology, sequencing, and reference laboratory confirmation remain essential. Management requires prompt supportive care, appropriate isolation, public health notification, and pathogen-directed antimicrobial, antiviral, antitoxin, or prophylactic strategies where available. By integrating clinical presentation, diagnostics, treatment, vaccines, and One Health considerations, this review aims to translate knowledge on emerging infections into practical bedside preparedness.

Driven magnetic 3D DNA walker coupled with CHA based on light-activatable hairpin: A dual-amplification strategy for ultrasensitive hantavirus RNA sensing.

Orthohantavirus dabieshanense (DBSV) is a newly emerging hantavirus and poses a potential threat to human health. Current detection methods based on polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays for other hantaviruses such as Hantaan virus (HTNV) and Seoul virus (SEOV) may carry the risk of false negatives. This study aims to address the technical bottlenecks of low accuracy and insufficient sensitivity in current detection of DBSV. We developed a signal amplification strategy mediated by a photoactivated magnetic 3D DNA walker, which enables light-controllable fluorescence detection. Specifically, this fluorescence sensing platform introduces the catalytic hairpin assembly (CHA) technique for dual signal amplification. This system requires dual activation by UV irradiation and target RNA. Then, exonuclease III (Exo III) provides propulsion for the walking process to release a specific trigger strand, subsequently activating the CHA cycle and producing a fluorescent signal. Experimental results confirm that the system displays excellent selectivity and anti-interference ability, successfully distinguishing target sequences with single-base mismatch (SBM), double-base mismatch (DBM), and triple-base mismatch (TBM). Furthermore, the platform achieves a DBSV-RNA detection limit (LOD) of 30 fM with a linear range spanning 10-1-106 pM. The applicability of the method has been rigorously validated using human serum specimens. This study provides a novel strategy for the application of spatiotemporally controllable DNA walkers in RNA detection, demonstrating considerable potential for early viral diagnosis and field monitoring.

Climate change as an amplifier of hantavirus risk in South Asia: a neglected nexus.

Hantavirus is seroepidemiologically established in South Asian human populations and rodent reservoirs, with anti-hantavirus antibodies confirmed in occupational risk groups in India, an independent association with chronic kidney disease demonstrated in Sri Lanka, and co-infection with leptospirosis identified in more than one-fifth of hospitalized leptospirosis patients. Despite this, the intersection of these findings with the region's intensifying monsoon floods, rapid urbanization, and climate-driven rodent ecology remains entirely unexamined. This letter highlights the neglected climate-hantavirus nexus, identifies structural vulnerabilities that amplify transmission risk in South Asia, and calls for integrated flood-response surveillance and One Health coordination across the region.
