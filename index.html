Design and in silico evaluation of PV-GNProtecta: an mRNA multi-epitope vaccine candidate against Puumala virus using immunoinformatics and structural analysis.

Puumala virus (PUUV), the principal causative agent of nephropathia epidemica (NE), poses a significant public health threat due to its zoonotic transmission, renal complications, and the lack of an approved vaccine. In this study, an immunoinformatics-driven mRNA multi-epitope vaccine, PV-GNProtecta, was designed using conserved regions of the viral glycoprotein (G) and nucleoprotein (N). Highly antigenic, non-allergenic, and non-toxic T-cell and B-cell epitopes were identified and assembled with appropriate adjuvants to enhance immunogenicity. Population coverage analysis suggested broad HLA responsiveness across diverse populations. The vaccine construct exhibited favorable physicochemical properties, including stability, solubility, and antigenicity. Structural validation predicted high model quality, with 95.5% of residues located in favored Ramachandran regions and an ERRAT score of 91.4. Molecular docking and MM-GBSA analyses predicted favorable computational interactions between PV-GNProtecta and TLR4, while a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation provided computational insights into the dynamic behavior of the vaccine-TLR4 complex. Codon optimization suggested compatibility of the designed sequence with an Escherichia coli-based expression framework. Immune simulation predicted potential humoral and cellular immune response patterns, including changes in IgG, IFN-γ, and IL-2 levels. The designed mRNA sequence exhibited favorable predicted secondary structural characteristics; however, further experimental evaluation is required to assess translational efficiency and stability. Although this study is limited to computational analyses and requires experimental validation, the findings suggest that PV-GNProtecta may represent a potential candidate for further investigation against PUUV infection.

[Preparedness for summer illnesses and emergencies in Chile, 2024-2025: lessons from an innovative policyPreparação para doenças e emergências no verão de 2024-2025 no Chile: lições de uma política inovadora].

Climate change and the increasing frequency of extreme heat events have heightened the importance of health issues during the summer season. This season is also associated with greater population mobility, outdoor recreational activities, and exposure to biological and environmental risks, all of which require coordinated responses from health systems. Within this context, the Ministry of Health of Chile implemented the summer seasonal illness and emergency preparedness plan 2024-2025, with the goal of strengthening surveillance, intersectoral coordination, and risk communication in response to priority threats to population health.This article describes the design, implementation, and key results of this first national initiative to prepare for diseases occurring during the summer season. The strategy was based on a multilevel governance model that liaised between the central, regional, and local levels of the health system through national coordination meetings, integrated surveillance, strengthening of technical capacities, and public communications. During the season covered by the plan, detection of Aedes aegypti remained confined to areas where it was previously known to occur; 395 outbreaks of foodborne illnesses affecting 2618 people were investigated; 8 cases of hantavirus were reported, with no deaths; and environmental risks and emergencies were monitored, including 4330 forest fires, none of which affected health facilities.This experience demonstrates the feasibility of implementing integrated disease preparedness strategies during the summer season and provides lessons for their consolidation as public policy, including the formalization of coordination mechanisms, the systematic assessment of implementation, and the incorporation of new hazards, such as drowning and injuries associated with recreational water activities. El cambio climático y la frecuencia creciente de eventos extremos asociados al calor han incrementado la relevancia de los temas de salud en la temporada estival. En este contexto, el Ministerio de Salud de Chile implementó el Plan de preparación para enfermedades y emergencias estacionales de verano 2024-2025, con el objetivo de fortalecer la vigilancia, la coordinación intersectorial y la comunicación de riesgo frente a amenazas prioritarias para la salud poblacional. La estrategia se sustentó en un modelo de gobernanza multinivel que articuló los niveles central, regional y local del sistema de salud, mediante sesiones nacionales de coordinación, vigilancia integrada, fortalecimiento de capacidades técnicas y vocerías públicas. Durante su implementación, se mantuvo la detección de Aedes aegypti circunscrita a zonas previamente conocidas, se investigaron 395 brotes de enfermedades transmitidas por alimentos que afectaron a 2 618 personas, se notificaron 8 casos de hantavirus, sin fallecidos, y se realizó el seguimiento de riesgos ambientales y emergencias, incluidos 4 330 incendios forestales, sin afectación de establecimientos de salud. Driven magnetic 3D DNA walker coupled with CHA based on light-activatable hairpin: A dual-amplification strategy for ultrasensitive hantavirus RNA sensing.

Orthohantavirus dabieshanense (DBSV) is a newly emerging hantavirus and poses a potential threat to human health. Current detection methods based on polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays for other hantaviruses such as Hantaan virus (HTNV) and Seoul virus (SEOV) may carry the risk of false negatives. This study aims to address the technical bottlenecks of low accuracy and insufficient sensitivity in current detection of DBSV. We developed a signal amplification strategy mediated by a photoactivated magnetic 3D DNA walker, which enables light-controllable fluorescence detection. Specifically, this fluorescence sensing platform introduces the catalytic hairpin assembly (CHA) technique for dual signal amplification. This system requires dual activation by UV irradiation and target RNA. Then, exonuclease III (Exo III) provides propulsion for the walking process to release a specific trigger strand, subsequently activating the CHA cycle and producing a fluorescent signal. Experimental results confirm that the system displays excellent selectivity and anti-interference ability, successfully distinguishing target sequences with single-base mismatch (SBM), double-base mismatch (DBM), and triple-base mismatch (TBM). Furthermore, the platform achieves a DBSV-RNA detection limit (LOD) of 30 fM with a linear range spanning 10-1-106 pM. The applicability of the method has been rigorously validated using human serum specimens. This study provides a novel strategy for the application of spatiotemporally controllable DNA walkers in RNA detection, demonstrating considerable potential for early viral diagnosis and field monitoring.

Vaccines Against Hantavirus Infections: A Systematic Literature Review

Background/Objectives: Hantaviruses infections are responsible for a hemorrhagic fever with renal and cardiopulmonary syndrome with case-fatality rates up to 50%. While inactivated vaccines are already available in China and South Korea, several additional candidate vaccines are under development. This systematic review summarizes the current clinical evidence on immunogenicity and short-term safety of vaccines targeting different hantavirus strains. Methods: Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, a systematic literature search was carried out to identify randomized and nonrandomized trials of any phase. Data were synthesized narratively within strata defined by vaccine platform, target virus, assay, immunogenicity and safety. Results: Ten trials on 961 adults were included: four single-arm trials on the inactivated vaccine and six early-phase DNA-vaccine trials. One month after last dose, the inactivated vaccine achieved neutralizing seropositivity by PRNT50 in 45.1% of the tested subjects after two priming doses and 55.7% after three, while the corresponding binding-antibody values measured by IFA were 87.3% and 95.7%. Among DNA vaccines, homologous responses reached 100% against HTNV and 83.3% against PUUV with monovalent constructs, whereas dual responses to the bivalent construct were registered in 33.3% and 77.8% of the subjects, when measured through PRNT50 and PsVNA50, respectively. One serious adverse event occurred, most likely unrelated to vaccination. Conclusions: No serious safety signal was identified, although safety remains insufficiently characterized. Measured immunogenicity depends as much on the assay as on the vaccine, and, in the absence of a validated correlate of protection, it cannot be read as a clinical benefit. Future research should prioritize validated correlates of protection and innovative trial designs adapted to low-incidence diseases.
